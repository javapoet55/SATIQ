Safety for students, parents and tutors

Last updated November 8, 2026

In an emergency, call 911. If a child is being exploited online, report it to the NCMEC CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678.

Prep Orbit connects students with volunteer tutors in their community, and the tutoring itself happens offline. We built privacy limits into the product, but we are honest about what we do not do: we do not run background checks on tutors, we do not verify their credentials, and we do not supervise sessions. A “Reviewed” badge means an administrator looked at a profile, nothing more. These steps are how you stay safe.

For students

  • Tell a parent, guardian or trusted adult before you ask a tutor for help and before every meeting. Show them the tutor’s profile and LinkedIn page.
  • Meet in public such as a library, school or busy café, during the day. Never meet at your home or the tutor’s home, and do not accept a ride.
  • Keep your details private. Tutors only see your first name and last initial until they accept, and your ZIP code is never shown. Do not share your home address, your daily schedule, passwords or photos.
  • Tutoring is always free. If anyone asks you for money, gifts or favors, say no and report it.
  • Trust your gut. Leave if something feels wrong, even if you cannot explain why.
  • Report and block from the Connections page at any time. You do not need a reason that feels “serious enough.”

For parents and guardians

  • Teens 13 and older may create accounts. Please know about your teen’s account and who they are connecting with.
  • When a tutor accepts, your teen’s email address and full name are shared with that tutor. Consider having your teen use an email address that you can also see.
  • Sit in on, or stay nearby for, the first session, and ask to see a tutor’s LinkedIn page and school. Because the school and LinkedIn details are not verified by us, check them yourself if it matters to you.
  • Prefer public locations and shorter first sessions. Online sessions should happen in a shared room of your home, not a bedroom.
  • Be alert to a tutor who asks for secrecy, wants to move to private messaging apps, sends gifts, asks for photos, or asks to meet somewhere private. Any of these is a reason to end the connection and report it.
  • You can ask us to review or delete your teen’s account at any time. See the Privacy Policy.

For tutors

  • Tutor for free. Never ask for or accept money or gifts.
  • Meet only in public places or in sessions that a parent or guardian can see. Never be alone with a minor in a private space, give rides, or contact a student outside what was agreed.
  • Do not ask for personal information, photos or social media accounts, and do not share your own home address or private details.
  • Keep communication appropriate, about the subject, and where a parent or guardian could read it.
  • Log only sessions that really happened. If a student does not confirm one, do not push them.
  • Your LinkedIn link and school are shown to signed-in students who search. Do not add details you are not comfortable with students and families seeing.

How Prep Orbit protects privacy

  • Others see only a first name and last initial until a request is accepted.
  • ZIP codes and exact locations are never shown; search shows a distance range.
  • Invitation emails contain display names and a link only.
  • Students can send a limited number of requests per day, and a tutor who declines is not re-asked immediately.
  • Either side can end a connection, block, or report in the app, and administrators review reports.

Reporting a problem

Use Report a problem on any connection in the app. You can also end the connection and block the person in the same step. Administrators review reports and may remove tutors or close accounts. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911 first.
